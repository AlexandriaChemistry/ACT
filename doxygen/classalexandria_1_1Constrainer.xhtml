alexandria::Constrainer Class Reference

#include <act/constraints/shake.h>

Description

Distance constraints using SHAKE and RATTLE.

Constrains bond lengths and, for angle constraints, the 1-3 distances of angles. All constraints are solved together using Newton iterations (matrix SHAKE).

Make the list of constraints.

With bond constraints, all bonds are constrained to the reference bond length. With angle constraints, the distance between the outer atoms i and k of each angle i-j-k is constrained as well, to the distance given by the reference bond lengths and angle. Around an atom with n bonded neighbours at most 2n-3 angles are constrained, since more would be redundant, and two around a planar atom with three neighbours. Angles of more than 175 degrees are not constrained either. Finally, an angle constraint is left out if it is (nearly) linearly dependent on the other constraints in the starting coordinates, for instance in rings.

Remove the force components along the constraints.

Forces of constraints are added such that the accelerations of the atoms do not change the constrained distances. The result is zero at a minimum of the energy under the constraints, for instance for the convergence check in energy minimization.

Execute the Shake algorithm as the first part of rattle.

The constraint forces act along the constrained pairs in the reference coordinates, that should satisfy the constraints. The displacements due to the constraints divided by the time step are added to the velocities, as in the first part of the rattle algorithm. See Andersen, J Comp Phys, 52, 24, 1983.

Execute the Shake algorithm.

The constraint equations of Ryckaert, Ciccotti and Berendsen, J Comp Phys, 23, 327, 1977 are solved for all constraints together by Newton iterations, as in the matrix SHAKE algorithm of Kraeutler, van Gunsteren and Huenenberger, J Comput Chem 22, 501,

  1. Unlike iterating over the constraints one at a time, this converges quickly for coupled constraints, as with angle constraints.
